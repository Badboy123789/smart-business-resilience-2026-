How We Work

An Engineering Approach Built Around Performance.

At Smart Business Resilience, our approach focuses on understanding project requirements, coordinating engineering disciplines and considering how buildings perform throughout their lifecycle.

Our Method

A Clear and Considered Way of Working

Good engineering begins with understanding the brief. A structured approach helps identify priorities, improve coordination and keep attention on the practical requirements of each project.

01 / UNDERSTAND

Understand the Brief

We begin by understanding the project objectives, building requirements, constraints and the needs of the people who will use the space.

02 / COORDINATE

Coordinate the Disciplines

Electrical, mechanical, public health, lighting and sustainability considerations need to work together as part of the wider building design.

03 / CONSIDER

Consider the Whole Lifecycle

Engineering decisions should consider usability, efficiency, maintenance and the long-term performance of building systems.

04 / REVIEW

Review the Requirements

Requirements and design information should be reviewed carefully to help identify coordination issues and clarify outstanding questions.

05 / REFINE

Refine the Design

Where necessary, design considerations can be refined to respond to project priorities, practical constraints and relevant performance objectives.

06 / SUPPORT

Support Project Objectives

The aim is to support coordinated engineering solutions that respond to the brief and the intended use of the building.

Our Principles

What Guides Our Approach

These principles describe the considerations that inform a thoughtful, engineering-focused approach to building design.

Functionality First

Building systems should respond to the practical needs of the space, its users and the activities taking place within it.

Coordination

Considering the relationship between engineering disciplines helps support a more coherent overall building design.

Efficiency

Energy use, system performance and operational requirements are important considerations when evaluating engineering solutions.

Long-Term Thinking

Maintainability, adaptability and lifecycle performance should be considered alongside immediate project requirements.

Integrated Thinking

Engineering Disciplines That Work Together

Buildings depend on interconnected systems. Electrical and mechanical installations, public health services, lighting and sustainability considerations can all influence the overall design.

Considering these relationships early can help teams identify dependencies and make informed decisions as project requirements develop.

  • Electrical engineering considerations
  • Mechanical building services
  • Public health engineering
  • Lighting design considerations
  • Sustainability and energy performance
  • Coordination across engineering disciplines

Looking Beyond the Drawings

People and Usability

Consider how building systems support comfort, safety and the day-to-day needs of occupants.

Building Performance

Consider how systems contribute to energy use, functionality and operational performance.

Practical Coordination

Recognise interfaces between systems and the importance of clear project information.

Future Considerations

Take account of maintainability, adaptability and longer-term building needs.

Collaboration

Working Towards the Same Objective

Successful engineering design depends on clear communication, shared project information and a common understanding of requirements.

01

Clear Communication

Keeping requirements and design considerations clear helps project stakeholders understand priorities and dependencies.

02

Joined-Up Thinking

Considering the interfaces between disciplines helps support a coordinated approach to building services.

03

Purposeful Decisions

Engineering decisions should be informed by the brief, practical constraints and the intended performance of the building.
