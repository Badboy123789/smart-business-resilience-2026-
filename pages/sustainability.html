Our Perspective

Considering the Bigger Picture

Sustainable engineering involves considering how building systems use energy, support occupants and perform throughout their operational life.

Electrical, mechanical, public health and lighting systems all influence building performance. Considering these systems together can help identify opportunities to improve efficiency and reduce unnecessary resource use.

Each project has different objectives and constraints, so sustainability considerations should be aligned with its specific requirements.

Key Considerations

Energy Performance

Understanding how building systems consume and use energy.

Resource Efficiency

Considering practical ways to reduce unnecessary resource use.

Occupant Comfort

Balancing performance with the needs of people using a building.

Long-Term Operation

Considering maintenance, adaptability and operational needs.

Areas of Consideration

Where Engineering Meets Sustainability

Different engineering disciplines contribute to how a building performs. These are key areas to consider when developing a sustainability-focused design.

01 / ENERGY

Energy Efficiency

Consider the energy requirements of building services and how system selection, controls and operation influence energy use.

02 / LIGHTING

Lighting Performance

Consider appropriate lighting levels, efficient lighting technologies, controls and opportunities to make better use of available daylight.

03 / MECHANICAL

Mechanical Systems

Consider heating, cooling, ventilation and controls in relation to building use, comfort and operational energy requirements.

04 / WATER

Water Efficiency

Consider water demand, efficient fittings and the practical requirements of public health engineering systems.

05 / INTEGRATION

Integrated Design

Consider how electrical, mechanical, lighting and public health systems interact so that design decisions support the wider building objectives.

06 / LIFECYCLE

Lifecycle Thinking

Consider maintenance, durability, adaptability and operational needs rather than focusing only on initial installation.

Building Performance

Making Informed Engineering Decisions

Building performance depends on several connected factors. Understanding these factors helps teams evaluate options against project objectives and practical constraints.

System Selection

Evaluate potential systems against building requirements, expected use, efficiency considerations and maintainability.

Controls and Operation

Consider how suitable controls and operating strategies may help systems respond to actual building needs.

Coordination Across Disciplines

Consider how decisions in one engineering discipline may affect the requirements and performance of another.

Practical Maintenance

Consider access, servicing requirements and the practical operation of building systems over time.

Long-Term Perspective

Sustainability Beyond Installation

The long-term performance of a building is influenced by its design, operation, maintenance and changing requirements.

Taking these factors into account can help project teams make informed decisions that reflect both immediate needs and future operational considerations.

Sustainability objectives should be realistic, measurable where appropriate and aligned with the particular needs of each project.

  • Operational energy considerations
  • Maintainability and servicing access
  • Adaptability to changing building needs
  • Appropriate lighting and controls
  • Water demand and resource efficiency
  • Coordination between building systems
A Considered Process

Putting Sustainability into Context

Sustainability considerations are most useful when they relate directly to the project brief and the intended performance of the building.

Understand

Establish project priorities, building use and relevant performance objectives.

Evaluate

Consider relevant engineering options, constraints and potential performance implications.

Coordinate

Consider how systems interact and how sustainability objectives relate to the wider design.
